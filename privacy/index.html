Privacy Policy

Last updated: 21 September 2026

This policy covers both Notesage apps — the iPhone app and the Mac app. They are built from one codebase but they do not behave identically, and where they differ this policy says so rather than averaging them.

The short version

Neither app collects anything. No account, no analytics, no crash reporting, no identifier. The analytics and crash-reporting libraries are not merely switched off — they are not compiled into either app at all, and there is no setting that could turn them on.

Your notes stay in the folder you chose. The one time note content leaves your device is when you use an AI feature on the Mac, and then it goes to the provider you configured — never to us.

There is no Notesage server. Not for your notes, not for diagnostics, not for anything. We have nothing to keep, nothing to lose, and nothing to hand over.

What Notesage stores, and where

Notesage reads and writes files only inside the folder you grant it access to — typically a folder in iCloud Drive, or one under On My iPhone. On iOS the system enforces this: the app cannot see anything outside that grant.

Kept on your device, outside your library:

  • The grant itself, as a security-scoped bookmark.
  • Your recently-read list, and your view, sort and grouping preferences.
  • On the Mac, your settings. Any API keys you enter for AI providers go into the macOS keychain, not into a file and not into your library folder, and are sent only to the provider they belong to.

Kept inside your own library folder, alongside your notes:

  • Your notes, folders, and anything you capture with the share sheet.
  • Your pinned items (.notesage/pins.json) and reading progress (.notesage/reading-progress.json), so the two apps agree.

None of this is transmitted to us, and we have no way to read any of it.

AI features (Mac only)

The Mac app can send text to an AI model. It goes to the provider you configured, under that provider's privacy policy and terms, using your own API key — there is no default provider, so until you set one up none of this happens at all.

Most of it happens only when you ask: a chat message, an editor action like Improve or Summarize, an agent run. Inline completions are the exception, and worth knowing about: if you have configured a connection for them, the app sends the text around your cursor about a third of a second after you stop typing, without you asking each time. Where that goes depends on the connection — a local model (Ollama, or the bundled one) keeps it on your machine; an API-key connection sends it to that provider. You can switch inline completions off in Settings.

Scheduled automations are the other case where nobody is present: if you set one up with an AI step, it runs on its schedule and calls the provider then.

Depending on what you invoke, that can include the text you selected, the document you are working in, and material the assistant is given access to. Treat it as you would pasting the same text into that provider's own app.

Providers are yours to choose and include Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, any OpenAI-compatible endpoint you point at, and any MCP servers you connect. Notesage can also run a model entirely on your machine — through Ollama or a bundled local model — in which case nothing leaves the device at all.

We never receive any of it. There is no Notesage server in this path; the app talks to your provider directly.

What Notesage does not do

  • No accounts, sign-in, or profiles — on either app.
  • No advertising, and no tracking across other apps or websites.
  • No selling or sharing of personal information.
  • No collection of any kind, by either app.
  • No reading of your notes by us, on any platform, ever.

Children

Notesage is a note-taking tool with no social features, no feeds, and no communication between users. Neither app collects anything from anyone, of any age.

Deleting your data

Your data is your files. Delete them as you would any files — in Notesage, in the Files app, or on your Mac. Removing the app's access (Change library folder) or deleting the app removes the grant and the local preferences; your notes stay where they are, because they were always yours.

There is nothing on our side to delete, because nothing reaches us.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version is published at the same URL with a new "last updated" date.

The substantive change on 21 September 2026: earlier versions of the Mac app could send anonymous usage and crash reports, off by default. That was removed from the code, and the accounts with both providers were closed.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to ask us for access to personal data we hold about you, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict how we process it, to receive it in a portable form, and to object to processing. Where processing rests on consent, you can withdraw it at any time.

In practice these rights have nothing to bite on here, and the reason is worth stating plainly rather than dressing up: no personal data reaches us at all. We hold nothing about you, so there is nothing to show you, correct, erase, restrict, port, or object to. Everything the app stores stays on your device, where you can inspect and delete it directly without asking us.

If you think we have handled your personal data wrongly, you can complain to a supervisory authority. In Sweden that is Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten (IMY), imy.se.
